Meal Pic Creative Brief

Content

Make great videos with Meal Pic in them. You know your audience better than we do, so you get full creative control. No script, no approval. The one must: show the app working on a real meal.

Priority 1: Promote app

Make the app a big part of the video, not a half-second cutaway. Show the magic moment: snap your food, the calories load, then edit them.

Priority 2: Get views

Make the best video you can so it reaches as many people as possible.

Views × how well you sell the app = money for you.

Examples of a good promotion

Why are these good promotions?

They show the magic moment.

Point the camera at food, the calories pop up. People see it and think “wait, what app is that?” That moment sells the app for you.

The app is the main character.

The whole video revolves around what the app shows. Take the app out and there’s no video.

The theme is something the RIGHT people care about.

Losing weight, getting lean, eating more protein, eating healthy. Those viewers download. The wrong viewers just watch.

Every integration MUST include 3 parts:

The theme

Grab the RIGHT people. Open a question they want answered, about something only people into working out, weight loss, calorie tracking or the gym would stop for.

The magic moment

Snap the photo, the calories and macros load, keep going. Do this in the first 15 seconds. Say in one line why it matters to you (“I stopped guessing,” “I’m finally losing weight without weighing my food”).

The CTA

Short, with a reason. “Link in my bio gets you 3 days free.”

Examples of a bad promotion

Example 2: a mukbang. The audience is there to watch someone eat 8,000 calories, not to count their own.

Why is this a bad promotion?

Wrong crowd.

It hooked comedy fans, not people who want to eat better. Big views, almost no downloads.

The app was a tangent.

It showed up for half a second and didn’t fit the video.

Video ideas

Anything gym / fitness / weight loss related

Day in the life

What I eat in a day

Scan every meal as you go, and the total adds up at the end.

“I tracked everything I ate for 7 days”

A day-in-my-life with Meal Pic at every meal, and what changed.

Guess the calories

Two meals that look the same (or a “healthy” one that isn’t), guess first, then scan to reveal.

Healthy / High-protein recipe

Cook it, plate it, scan it at the end to show the macros.

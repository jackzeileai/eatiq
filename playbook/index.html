Meal Pic Creative Brief

6 min read

In this brief, we’ll teach you the most important parts of making a successful Meal Pic video.

Your 2 priorities

1. Promote the app

  • Make the app a big part of the video, not a half-second cutaway.
  • Show the magic moment: 1. scan your food, 2. load the calories, 3. edit if needed.

2. Get views

  • Make the best video you can so it reaches as many people as possible.
  • 10K views from the right audience beat 1M random viewers.
How well you
promote the app×Views=$$$for you

Every integration should include 3 parts

1. The theme

  • Get the RIGHT people interested.
  • Make the topic of the video something a person who’d download a calorie tracker cares about, like:
    • Fitness
    • Weight loss
    • Healthy meals
    • Calorie counting
    • High-protein eating

2. The magic moment

  • Show the app on screen: scan your food, the calories load, edit if needed.
  • Do this in the first 15 seconds.
  • Say a line about why you use it: “I use Meal Pic because all I have to do is take a picture and it tracks all the calories for me.”

3. The CTA

  • End the video with a “why.”
  • “Click the link in my bio so you get 3 days free.”

Why are these good videos?

1. The theme is something the RIGHT people care about.

  • Losing weight, getting lean, eating more protein, eating healthy.
  • Those viewers download.
  • The wrong viewers just watch.

2. The app is the main character.

  • The app is an important part of the video.
  • It solves a problem and is actually useful in the video.

3. They show the magic moment.

  • Scan your food, the calories load, edit if needed.
  • People see it and think “wait, what app is that?”
  • That moment sells the app for you.

Examples of a bad video

  • Wrong crowd. It hooked comedy fans, not people who want to track calories.
  • The app was a tangent. The app didn’t really have anything to do with the video.
  • Wrong crowd. The audience is there to watch someone eat 8,000 calories, not to count their own.
  • She talked about the app well. The viewers just weren’t the right people.
  • Wrong crowd. Novelty food pulls people who want a laugh, not people who want to eat better.
  • The app was a tangent. The app didn’t really have anything to do with the video.

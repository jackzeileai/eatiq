1. The hook

The first two seconds. It has two jobs, and it needs both.

Job 1 · Get interest

Open a loop.

Make them need to know what happens next. Curiosity, shock, or a story that's already moving.

  • Start in the action. No intro.
  • Text on screen that asks a question or makes a claim.
  • Keep it clean. One thing on screen, not five.
Job 2 · Get the right person

Talk to people who track food.

A hook that pulls in everyone gets views, but nobody downloads. The best steak in the world won't sell to a room full of vegans. Hook the people who care about food, fitness and health.

Hormozi's rule: a hook is a call out (this is for you) plus a promise (watch this and you'll get something).

2. The meat

Show the app doing the thing. This is the moment people download.

1

Snap the food

One photo. Your real plate.

2

Macros load

Calories, protein, carbs, fat. On screen.

3

Fix anything

Tap to edit. Show that you can.

The magic moment

Snap → macros → edit. Every video.

Screen-record the real thing on your real food. Keep it on screen long enough to read. Talk over it, or let the text do it.

  • Show all three steps, 3 seconds or more.
  • Your real food, your real phone.
  • Say what it did for you, in one line.
  • A one-second flash of the app icon.
  • “I use Meal Pic” with nothing shown.
  • Listing features instead of showing one.
The anti-value trick

“Here's why I'm deleting Meal Pic.”

“It's not because it scans my food in one photo.”

“It's not because it gets my macros right.”

“It's not because I can fix anything with a tap.”

“It's because I have no excuse anymore.”

Every “not because” is a feature. The viewer hears the value three times before the punchline.

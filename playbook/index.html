Meal Pic Creative Brief

6 min read

In this brief, we’ll teach you the most important parts of making a successful Meal Pic video.

Your 2 priorities

1. Promote the app

  • Make the app a big part of the video, not a half-second cutaway.
  • Show the magic moment: snap your food, the calories load, then edit them.

2. Get views

  • Make the best video you can so it reaches as many people as possible.
  • 10K views from the right audience beat 1M random viewers.

How well you promote the app × views = $$$ for you.

Every integration MUST include 3 parts:

1. The theme

  • Get the RIGHT people interested.
  • Make the video about something a person who’d download a calorie tracker cares about.

2. The magic moment

  • Snap the photo, the calories and macros load, keep going.
  • Do this in the first 15 seconds.
  • Say in one line why it matters to you (“I stopped guessing,” “I’m finally losing weight without weighing my food”).

3. The CTA

  • Short, with a “why.”
  • “Click the link in my bio so you get 3 days free.”

Examples of a good video

Why are these good videos?

1. They show the magic moment.

  • Point the camera at food, the calories pop up.
  • People see it and think “wait, what app is that?”
  • That moment sells the app for you.

2. The app is the main character.

  • The whole video revolves around what the app shows.
  • Take the app out and there’s no video.

3. The theme is something the RIGHT people care about.

  • Losing weight, getting lean, eating more protein, eating healthy.
  • Those viewers download.
  • The wrong viewers just watch.

Examples of a bad video

Example 2: a mukbang. The audience is there to watch someone eat 8,000 calories, not to count their own.

Why are these bad videos?

1. Wrong crowd.

  • It hooked comedy fans, not people who want to eat better.
  • Big views, almost no downloads.

2. The app was a tangent.

  • It showed up for half a second and didn’t fit the video.

Video ideas

1. Anything gym / fitness / weight loss related

2. Day in the life

3. What I eat in a day

Scan every meal as you go, and the total adds up at the end.

4. “I tracked everything I ate for 7 days”

A day-in-my-life with Meal Pic at every meal, and what changed.

5. Guess the calories

Two meals that look the same (or a “healthy” one that isn’t), guess first, then scan to reveal.

6. Healthy / High-protein recipe

Cook it, plate it, scan it at the end to show the macros.
